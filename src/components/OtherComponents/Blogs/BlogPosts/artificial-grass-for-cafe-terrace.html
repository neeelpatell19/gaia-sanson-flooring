An outdoor terrace can be the busiest part of a café or restaurant in India's cooler months and its evening trade in summer. Artificial grass turns a bare rooftop or concrete patio into a green, garden-like setting that photographs well and feels relaxed, without the watering and mud of a real lawn. Commercial use asks more of it than a home balcony does: chairs dragged across it all day, spilled drinks, cigarette ends, heavy rain and the afternoon sun. Café terrace grass at a glance

  • Pile height: short, about 20 to 35 mm, so tables and chairs sit level and do not track.
  • Shade: umbrellas, pergolas or sails over seating, for comfort and a cooler surface.
  • Drainage: a slab that falls to clear outlets, kept free of debris.
  • Cleaning: sweep and rinse daily; deal with spills and food at once.
  • Zoning: hard paths for service routes, grass for seating and lounging.

Is artificial grass good for a restaurant terrace?

Yes, when it is used for the right parts of the terrace. Grass works best in seating and lounge areas, around planters and in corners where guests linger. Main service routes from the kitchen, bar and entrance take constant traffic, trolleys and spills, so they are better in a hard, easily washed surface, with grass alongside. That zoning keeps the green look where guests see it and the practical surface where staff need it. A short one, around 20 to 35 mm. Chairs and tables sit level on a short pile and do not sink or leave tracks, and food, ash and dust sweep out easily. Longer piles look lush but lean and mat under furniture and hold debris. How do you lay artificial grass on a restaurant terrace?

On a sound slab that drains. Check the waterproofing and repair it if needed, because the grass will sit on it for years. Confirm the slab falls to its outlets by pouring water on it, and correct any ponding. Keep the grass back from outlets, or cut neat openings round them, and lay it in pieces that can be lifted to inspect the roof. On rooftops, have a structural engineer confirm the load of grass, planters, furniture and guests. Never fix anything through the waterproofing. How do you keep terrace grass clean during service?

With a short daily routine. Sweep or use a leaf blower after each service to remove food, napkins and cigarette ends. Rinse the grass daily in hot weather, and blot spills straight away, then rinse. Brush the pile against the grain weekly where chairs move most. Keep ashtrays on tables and away from the grass, because a cigarette can melt the fibres. Keep the wet zones around drinks stations on a hard surface; the UK Health and Safety Executive's guidance on slips in catering is a reminder that spills on any floor need prompt clean-up. Café terrace grass questions

Can restaurant chairs be used on artificial grass?

Yes. A short pile of about 20 to 35 mm keeps chairs and tables level and stops them leaving tracks.

Does artificial grass smell on a restaurant terrace?

Not if spills are rinsed away and the slab drains. Daily rinsing in hot weather keeps it fresh.

Is artificial grass a fire risk on a terrace?

Dropped cigarettes and embers can melt the fibres. Keep ashtrays on tables and grills and heaters well away from the grass.

How long does it take to lay grass on a café terrace?

Once the slab and drainage are sound, an average terrace can usually be laid in a day or two, so it can be scheduled between services or on a closed day.

Can artificial grass be used indoors in a café?

Yes, in dry areas such as feature corners and kids' zones. Keep it away from kitchens, bars and wet zones, where a washable hard floor is better.
