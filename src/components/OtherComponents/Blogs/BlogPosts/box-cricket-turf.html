Box cricket has become a hugely popular way to play in Indian cities. Netted arenas on rooftops, empty plots and school grounds are booked from early morning until late at night by office teams, friends and leagues who cannot find a full ground. For owners, the surface is the biggest single decision: it decides how the ball behaves, how players feel, how often the arena needs maintenance and whether it can earn from other sports in the off-hours. This article sets out what to plan.

Box cricket turf at a glance

  • Size: no official standard; size the box to the plot and the format you want to run.
  • Surface: short, dense turf for a predictable bounce with tennis or rubber balls.
  • Shared use: football turf lets one box host cricket and 5-a-side.
  • Base: level, compacted and sloped to drain, so the bounce stays true.
  • Netting: on all sides and overhead where balls can leave the box.

What size is a box cricket ground?

There is no official size, which is why arenas vary so much. Playo's guide to box cricket ground and pitch dimensions lists compact boxes of around 40 to 50 ft long and 25 to 30 ft wide, a shortened pitch, and net heights of about 10 to 12 ft indoors and 20 to 25 ft outdoors; many commercial arenas are larger. In practice, the plot decides. Measure the usable area, then plan the box, the pitch strip, a run-off margin inside the nets, a walkway, and space for players waiting and for a scoreboard. If the box will also host football, check the size against the 5-a-side guidance in football turf for a 5-a-side pitch.

Does box cricket turf need infill?

Longer football turf usually does; short court turf needs much less or none. Infill, normally sand with or without a performance granule, holds the fibres upright and controls bounce and grip. On a cricket box, too much loose infill can make the ball skid and scatter granules when it pitches, so the amount and type need care. What base does a box cricket turf need?

A firm, level base that drains, because any bump shows up as an unpredictable bounce. On open ground, that means excavating, laying and compacting a granular sub-base, and finishing it level with a gentle fall so rain runs off. On an existing concrete slab or a rooftop, repair cracks and hollows, check the fall to the drains, and protect the waterproofing. A rooftop arena also needs a structural engineer's confirmation that the slab can carry the turf, infill, netting posts and players. Sport England's outdoor surfaces guidance shows how much any sports surface depends on what is built beneath it. Installation is covered in how to install artificial grass.

How do you keep a box cricket turf playing well?

Brush it regularly so the fibres stand up, clear leaves and litter, and check the pitch area, where the ball lands most, for wear and loose infill. Rinse it during dusty spells and after events, and keep drains and edges clear before the monsoon. Rotating the pitch position, where the box allows, spreads wear. Box cricket turf questions

Can box cricket be played on football turf?

Yes. Many arenas use football turf so the same box hosts cricket and 5-a-side. A shorter pile, such as 30 mm, gives a firmer bounce.

Can a box cricket arena be built on a rooftop?

Yes, if a structural engineer confirms the slab can carry the load and the waterproofing and drainage are protected.

How high should box cricket nets be?

It depends on the site and format. Playo's guide suggests about 10 to 12 ft indoors and 20 to 25 ft outdoors; many outdoor arenas also net overhead.

Can a box cricket arena be used for other sports?

Yes. With football turf, the same box can host 5-a-side football and casual games, which helps fill bookings outside peak cricket hours.
