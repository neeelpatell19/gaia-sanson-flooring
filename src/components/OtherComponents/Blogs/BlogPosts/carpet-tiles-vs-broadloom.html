Carpet tiles or broadloom? Choose carpet tiles for offices and any space where layouts change, floors need spot repairs or access is needed beneath. Choose broadloom for hotel corridors, guest rooms, boardrooms and ballrooms, where one continuous, custom-designed floor matters more than replacing pieces.

Both are carpet, and both make a room quieter and softer underfoot than a hard floor. The difference is format. One is a kit of small modular pieces, the other a wide roll laid wall to wall, and that single difference changes how the floor is installed, repaired, designed and paid for over its life. Deciding room by room, rather than for a whole building at once, usually gives the best result.

How the two formats differ

  • Format: GAIA carpet tiles are 50 × 50 cm squares or 25 × 100 cm planks; broadloom comes in wide rolls.
  • Look: tiles show a grid that can be hidden or used in the design; broadloom gives a continuous surface with few seams.
  • Repairs: a damaged tile is lifted and swapped; broadloom needs a patch or a new section.
  • Change: tiles can be lifted and re-laid when a layout changes; broadloom is laid once.
  • Design: tiles mix colours and layouts; broadloom carries a bespoke pattern across a whole room.

What is the difference between carpet tiles and broadloom?

How the carpet arrives and goes down. Carpet tiles are small, stable pieces with a firm backing, laid one by one so that each can come up again. Broadloom is tufted in a wide roll, cut to the room on site and fitted as one piece or a few, joined at seams. GAIA makes both in India: GAIA carpet tiles across a large range of collections, and GAIA broadloom made to order in fibre, backing, texture, colour and pattern.

Which is easier to install and replace?

Carpet tiles, on both counts. Tiles arrive in boxes that fit in a lift, can be laid around occupied areas a section at a time, and can be cut and fitted by a small team. A damaged tile is simply swapped. Broadloom needs space to unroll and cut, careful seaming, and a patch repair that has to be matched to the surrounding pile. In a large open room, though, broadloom goes down quickly and leaves very few joins.

Which looks better?

It depends on the look you want. Broadloom suits rooms where the floor itself is the design, such as hotel corridors and ballrooms. Carpet tiles suit designs built from the format: monolithic floors where the grid nearly disappears, quarter-turn textures, colour blocks that mark out zones, or planks laid in herringbone. Is one quieter than the other?

Not in any way that should decide the choice. Both cushion footsteps and chair movement and stop the floor reflecting sound the way tile or stone does; the difference between them is small next to the difference between carpet and a hard floor, set out in carpet tiles versus vinyl. Choose on repairs, change and design instead.

Which costs less over the life of the floor?

Carpet tiles usually cost less over time where layouts change; broadloom where rooms stay the same. Worn tiles near doors and desks are replaced individually, and tiles move with the teams when a floor is replanned. Broadloom earns its keep in rooms that are rarely reconfigured and benefit from a bespoke design. Our team will price both formats for the same floor plan if you want a direct comparison.

Can you switch from broadloom to carpet tiles later?

Yes, and refurbishments are the usual moment to do it. The old broadloom and its adhesive come up, the subfloor is cleaned and levelled, and tiles go down in its place. Plan the change for when a floor is being replanned anyway, because the same work that clears the old carpet also frees the room for new furniture and cabling.

Carpet tiles or broadloom: room by room

  • Open-plan offices and workstations: carpet tiles.
  • Offices with raised access floors: carpet tiles, so floor panels can be lifted.
  • Meeting rooms and cabins: carpet tiles; broadloom in a boardroom that should feel bespoke.
  • Hotel corridors and guest rooms: broadloom.
  • Ballrooms, banquet halls and auditorium foyers: broadloom.
  • Retail, education and co-working spaces that change often: carpet tiles.

Format questions

Can carpet tiles and broadloom be used in the same building?

Yes, and it is common. A hotel might use broadloom in corridors and guest rooms with carpet tiles in back-of-house offices; an office might use tiles on the floor plate and broadloom in the boardroom.

Can broadloom be laid on a raised access floor?

It is rarely a good idea, because the carpet would have to be cut or lifted to reach the panels beneath. Carpet tiles are the usual choice there.
