To clean artificial grass, clear leaves and litter weekly, hose off dust, and brush the blades against their lean every month so they stand upright. Lift pet waste and spills straight away, wash the spot with mild soap and water, and deep-clean the whole lawn after the monsoon.

Artificial grass is low-maintenance, not no-maintenance. In Indian cities the work is mostly about dust, leaves and the occasional festival: construction dust settles on a terrace in days, pollen and leaves collect along the edges, Holi colour and Diwali debris arrive once a year, and pets use the lawn every day. None of it is difficult. Twenty minutes a week and a slightly longer clean a few times a year keep a lawn looking new and smelling fresh, and make it last longer.

Cleaning routine at a glance

  • Weekly: blow or rake off leaves and litter; spot-clean any mess.
  • Fortnightly in dry months: rinse off dust with a hose.
  • Monthly: brush against the grain so the blades stand up.
  • After the monsoon: clear drains and edges, check for weeds and moss.
  • Straight away: pet waste, food, drinks and anything sticky.

How often should artificial grass be cleaned?

A light clean every week, a brush every month, and a deep clean two or three times a year. Lawns under trees, on dusty sites or used by pets need more; a shaded balcony used for a morning cup of tea needs less. The test is simple: if leaves are lying on the surface or the blades look flat and dull, it is time.

What do you need to clean artificial grass?

  • a leaf blower, or a rake with plastic or rubber tines;
  • a stiff brush with synthetic bristles, or a power brush for large lawns;
  • a garden hose with a spray nozzle;
  • mild liquid soap or detergent, and a bucket;
  • paper towels and a plastic scraper for spills;
  • an enzyme cleaner made for pet urine, if you have pets.

Avoid metal rakes and wire brushes: they snag and pull the blades.

How do you get rid of leaves, dust and litter?

Blow or rake them off before they break down. Leaves left on the surface rot into the pile, hold moisture and stain the blades, and fine organic debris is where weeds and moss take hold. A leaf blower on a low setting is the fastest tool. Then rinse the lawn to wash dust down through the backing; in the dry months before the monsoon, a fortnightly rinse keeps the colour bright on dusty terraces.

How do you deal with pet waste and smells?

Lift solid waste as soon as you see it, then hose the spot. For urine, rinse the area thoroughly with water every few days, more often in hot weather when smells develop faster. If an odour persists, an enzyme cleaner sold for pet areas breaks down the residue rather than masking it. Make sure the area drains well: a lawn on a flat terrace or balcony needs a clear path to the outlet, which is covered in our piece on rooftop lawns.

How do you remove stains from artificial grass?

Act quickly and work gently. For food and drinks, scrape off solids, blot the liquid, then wash the spot with mild soap and warm water and rinse well. For chewing gum or wax, harden it with an ice cube and lift it off with a plastic scraper. For oil or grease, blot first, then use a little mild detergent. Always try a cleaner on a small corner first, and rinse everything off afterwards so no residue is left in the pile.

Why should you brush artificial grass?

To keep it looking like a lawn. Feet, furniture and rain flatten the blades over time, especially along paths and under chairs. Brushing against the direction the blades lean lifts them back up, spreads any infill evenly and stops matted patches forming. Once a month is enough for most homes; move furniture around now and then so the same patch is not pressed down for months.

Lawns with furniture, sports surfaces and lounge areas all stay newer for longer with a weekly routine.

What about Holi colour and Diwali debris?

Protect it first, then wash it. Before Holi, cover the lawn with a sheet or keep colour play on paving, because dry powder works deep into the pile. Afterwards, blow off as much dry colour as possible before wetting it, then rinse repeatedly with plenty of water. For Diwali, keep diyas, candles and fireworks off the grass: hot embers and flames melt synthetic blades and leave permanent marks. Sweep up debris the next morning.

Can you pressure wash or vacuum artificial grass?

Use both with care. A pressure washer on a low setting, held well away from the surface, can rinse a dusty lawn, but a close, powerful jet can loosen the backing and wash out infill. A household vacuum works on short-pile grass indoors or on a balcony; on longer piles a leaf blower is more effective. For anything beyond routine care, follow the maintenance instructions supplied with your grass; for larger installations, the maintenance guidelines published by the Synthetic Turf Council are a useful reference.

Busy surfaces need more. Sport England's guidance on artificial surfaces for outdoor sport covers maintenance for heavily used pitches and courts, and the lesson carries over to a home: a planned routine of brushing and debris removal is what keeps synthetic grass performing. A busy terrace or a school play area deserves the same approach on a smaller scale.

What should you never use on artificial grass?

  • metal rakes, wire brushes or sharp tools;
  • strong solvents, undiluted bleach or acids;
  • weedkillers sprayed straight onto the blades, unless the label allows it;
  • anything hot: barbecue coals, cigarettes, diyas or fireworks.

Artificial grass cleaning questions

Can you use soap on artificial grass?

Yes. Mild liquid soap or detergent in water is the safest everyday cleaner. Rinse it off thoroughly so no residue stays in the pile.

Does artificial grass need watering?

Not to stay green. It only needs rinsing to wash away dust and pet urine, and occasionally to cool the surface on a hot afternoon.

How often should artificial grass be brushed?

Every month for most home lawns, and every week on busy paths, play areas and sports surfaces. Brush against the lean of the blades so they stand upright.

How do you stop artificial grass smelling of pet urine?

Rinse the area with plenty of water after use, and wash it weekly with a mild enzyme cleaner made for artificial turf. Good drainage under the grass prevents most odour.
